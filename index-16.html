Understanding the First Step Act: What It Means for You
The First Step Act (FSA), signed into law in December 2018, is one of the most significant federal criminal justice reform bills in decades. If you or someone you love is in the federal system, this law may directly affect your release date.

What Is the First Step Act?

The First Step Act was designed to reduce recidivism, reform federal sentencing, and give incarcerated people more opportunities to earn their way home sooner. It applies to people in federal Bureau of Prisons (BOP) custody.

Earned Time Credits (ETCs)

You can now earn 10–15 days of time credits for every 30 days of successfully completing approved programming including education, vocational training, and drug treatment.

  • Low/minimum risk inmates: 15 days per 30 days of programming
  • Medium/high risk inmates: 10 days per 30 days of programming
  • Credits can be applied toward early transfer to supervised release or halfway house

RDAP — Drug Treatment Program

The Residential Drug Abuse Program (RDAP) can reduce your sentence by up to 12 months if you complete it and meet eligibility requirements. It's one of the most powerful tools available inside.

Retroactive Sentencing Reductions

The FSA made the Fair Sentencing Act of 2010 retroactive — meaning people sentenced under old crack cocaine laws may be eligible for sentence reductions. Contact a federal public defender or attorney immediately if this applies to you.

Good Time Credits Fix

The FSA fixed a calculation error that was shortchanging federal inmates — changing good time credits from 47 days per year to the full 54 days per year.

What You Should Do

  • Ask your case manager about your FSA eligibility score (PATTERN score)
  • Enroll in every approved program available to you
  • Track your earned time credits — request your records
  • Consult a reentry attorney if you believe you qualify for a sentence reduction

Starting a Box Truck Business with a Record in Ohio
A box truck business is one of the most accessible paths to self-employment for returning citizens. You don't need a degree, a perfect credit score, or a clean record — you need a plan.

Why Box Trucks?

Box truck businesses have low barriers to entry, high demand, and multiple income streams. Haul freight, do last-mile delivery, move furniture, or partner with Amazon Relay, GoShare, and Dolly.

Step 1 — Form Your LLC

  • File at ohiosos.gov — $99 online
  • Get a free EIN at irs.gov
  • Open a business bank account

Step 2 — CDL Requirements

Most box trucks under 26,000 lbs GVWR do not require a CDL — just a regular driver's license. Over 26,000 lbs requires a Class B CDL minimum.

Step 3 — FMCSA & DOT Numbers

If you cross state lines or haul for hire, you need a USDOT number and MC number. Register free at fmcsa.dot.gov.

Step 4 — Insurance

Budget $300–$600/month for commercial trucking insurance. Shop carriers that work with new authorities — Progressive, Travelers, and others.

Step 5 — Get Loads

  • Amazon Relay — consistent loads, requires MC number
  • GoShare — local delivery, easy entry
  • Dolly — furniture moving, great to start
  • DAT / Truckstop — load boards for freight

Does a Record Stop You?

Having a record does not automatically disqualify you from a CDL or operating a trucking business in Ohio. There are no blanket restrictions on business ownership.

Finding Housing in Ohio After Release: A Practical Guide
Housing is the #1 challenge for returning citizens. Without a stable place to live, everything else becomes harder. Here's a realistic breakdown of your options in Ohio.

First 30 Days — Immediate Options

  • Halfway Houses (RRCs) — Ask your BOP case manager about placement up to 12 months before release
  • Daybreak Dayton — 937-461-0566
  • Volunteers of America Ohio — 1-800-862-8845

Section 8 / Housing Choice Vouchers

Most felonies do NOT automatically disqualify you — housing authorities make individual determinations. Apply immediately upon release as waitlists are 1–3 years.

  • Dayton: Greater Dayton Premier Management — 937-910-7500

Transitional Housing

  • Volunteers of America Ohio — statewide
  • St. Vincent de Paul Dayton — 937-222-5555
  • Ohio Benefit Bank — 1-800-648-1176

Renting Privately with a Record

  • Be upfront about your situation before they find it
  • Offer a larger security deposit
  • Bring references from employers, case managers, or clergy
  • Target individual landlords over large property companies
  • Search "second chance apartments" in your area

Know Your Rights

Ohio law prohibits blanket bans on renting to people with records. If denied unfairly, contact Legal Aid Society of Southwest Ohio — 937-228-8088.
